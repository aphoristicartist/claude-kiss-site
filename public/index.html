01

The principle

Keep the Claude Code you value. Choose what surrounds it.

Claude KISS does not replace the model or build another agent runtime. It controls the layer where prompts, tools, integrations, and handoffs become ambient defaults. KISS points at the harness, never at the quality of the work.

  • Same Claude Code.The installed binary, your account, and your sessions stay in place.
  • Your boundaries.Questions stay questions; small fixes stay small; unrelated work stays yours.
  • Capability on purpose.Add web, skills, delegation, or MCP when the task actually needs them.

04

The research

A small relevant surface is a reliability choice.

Recent 2026 studies do not prove that one launcher is ideal. They do show that instruction count, visible tools, handoffs, and interface design change how coding agents behave.

  1. Aug 18A Jagged Frontier

    Across two scaffolds and four frontier models, the simpler mini-SWE-agent scaffold was more robust to semantics-preserving repository changes than OpenCode.

  2. Aug 11The Devil Is in the Interface

    Across 11,700 trajectories, useful structured tools improved consistency and Python CodeAct cut steps and tokens; scratchpad-style scaffolding had little effect.

  3. Aug 11Why Does CLAUDE.md Keep Growing?

    Across 247,694 instruction lifetimes, agent prompts grew 226%; preserving rationale enabled dramatic pruning and improved instruction following by up to 23.1%.

  4. Aug 2Deep Agentic Search

    For repository questions, direct retrieval scored 65.2% versus 46.2% for planner-to-subagent search; each correct answer cost less than half as much, and 41.8% of failures happened at handoff.

  5. Jul 31Instruction Stacking Collapse

    Instruction adherence fell from about 96% with one simultaneous rule to as low as 20% with twenty. More permanent policy can mean less policy followed.

  6. May 23How Many Tools Should an LLM Agent See?

    An adaptive system exposed about seven tools instead of fifty while retaining nearly identical retrieval coverage: 90.3% versus 90.8%.

The limit is part of the claim. Structured tools can outperform Bash-only minimalism, and difficult work can justify orchestration. The evidence supports default-minimal and task-relative—not “complexity is always bad.”

Trust boundary

Strong defaults are not a sandbox.

Bash remains powerful. User, project, and local settings—and their hooks and plugins—still load. Explicit native flags can replace managed defaults. Tool limits shape attention, not filesystem access. Review permissions, repository settings, plugins, and MCP configurations as code.
